## What $10-a-year hosting doesn't include

You're buying raw, unmanaged compute — that's the trade behind the price. Concretely:

- **No hand-holding.** Setup, security patches, and troubleshooting are on you. Competitors like IONOS advertise 24/7 expert support and a 99.99%+ availability target on their VPS line; RackNerd's budget plans assume you can run a server yourself.
- **cPanel isn't included.** You get a SolusVM-based control panel with a VNC console, OS rebuild, and rDNS settings. cPanel is a paid add-on if your workflow requires it.
- **Transfer is metered.** The quotas run 1–20 TB per month on a 1 Gbps port. For a cached, text-heavy website a few terabytes is far more than you'll use; for video or large-file serving, it's a real ceiling. IONOS, by contrast, sells unlimited traffic on its VPS plans.
- **SSD, not NVMe.** The budget tiers use standard SSD storage. NVMe appears on pricier competitors — Hostinger's KVM line and DigitalOcean's premium droplets both advertise it. For small sites the difference is minor; for I/O-heavy databases it can matter.
- **Fewer locations than the big clouds.** RackNerd covers US metros plus Amsterdam. DigitalOcean's shared-CPU plans alone span 19 regions. If latency to a specific city is critical, verify the dropdown first.

## Matching a plan to your workload

RAM is the constraint that decides most of this, so pick by memory footprint first and everything else falls into place.

- **768 MB ($9.89/yr) and 1.5 GB ($19.69/yr):** always-on light services. WireGuard or other VPN endpoints, uptime monitors, cron jobs, small bots, a personal proxy. The 1.5 GB tier adds enough room for one low-traffic WordPress install.
- **2.5 GB ($29.99/yr):** the default choice for a single real website. One WordPress site with caching, a small LEMP stack, a Minecraft server for a few friends, or two or three small containers.
- **3–4 GB ($38.99–$46.99/yr):** WordPress plus MySQL without constant swap pressure, small e-commerce, a handful of Docker services, or dev/staging environments.
- **5–8 GB ($58.99–$79.99/yr):** small production apps, self-hosted tool suites (Git, wiki, monitoring), busier forums.
- **10–16 GB ($99.99–$152.99/yr):** multiple apps on one box, medium databases, busier community sites.
- **20 GB+ ($209.99+/yr):** heavier combined stacks. Note these remain shared-core plans — sustained CPU work like video encoding or machine learning wants dedicated cores, which is what DigitalOcean's CPU-Optimized line sells starting at $42/month for 2 dedicated vCPUs.

When does a competitor genuinely fit better? Three cases, all info-only:

- You need licensed Windows Server. IONOS offers Windows Server 2022 or 2025 as a plan option; RackNerd's budget KVM line targets Linux.
- You want someone else to handle patches, security, and 3 a.m. problems. Managed plans exist across the market, but budget roughly double, as the DigitalOcean/Cloudways numbers above show.
- You need hourly billing, snapshots, and a full cloud API. DigitalOcean, Vultr, and Linode all do this with flat renewals — the index shows $20–24/month for 4 GB-class instances with 0% increases.

**Can I pay monthly instead of yearly?**
Yes, through the standard catalog, but the rates are several times higher than the annual specials. Monthly billing suits short-term testing; annual prepay suits everything else.

**Is this beginner-friendly?**
Only if you're comfortable in a terminal. There's no managed support layer, no included cPanel, and no one to call when Apache won't start. If you want vendor hand-holding, the managed hosts cost roughly double — that's the honest price of the service, whichever provider you choose.

**What about Windows?**
RackNerd's budget KVM lineup is built for Linux workloads. If a licensed Windows Server is a hard requirement, IONOS sells VPS plans with Windows Server 2022 or 2025 preinstalled.

**Is 1 TB of monthly transfer enough for a website?**
For a cached blog or business site, almost certainly — well-optimized text-heavy sites rarely exceed a few hundred gigabytes. Video streaming, large downloads, or image-heavy galleries need more headroom, either from RackNerd's higher tiers or from competitors advertising unmetered transfer such as HostGator, Bluehost, and DreamHost.
